Six steps. One case number from start to finish.

  1. Report

    A photo and a live location on WhatsApp. A voice note works too, in Konkani, Marathi, Hindi or English.

  2. Triage

    Claude reads the photo: species, urgency, aggression risk, and two or three safe things to do. It never names a diagnosis.

  3. Merge duplicates

    Another report within 200 m and 2 hours of a similar open case joins it. Both people get the updates.

  4. Alert the nearest team

    The zone's partner team gets the photo, a map link and two buttons: Accept or Decline.

  5. Escalate

    Closed, declined, or 10 minutes of silence: the next team is alerted, then the taluka officer.

  6. Close the loop

    Accepted, on site, resolved. Every reporter hears each step, then rates how it went.

Under the hood.

One Python service and one database. WhatsApp, Telegram and the demo above all become the same message, so they run the same logic.

Triage never blocks a rescue

If the model is slow or fails, the case is routed at high urgency and flagged for a person.

Save, then send

A message that cannot be delivered never undoes a case. A job picks up anything that stalled.

One state machine

Every change of state is checked against a table and written to an audit log.

Deadlines in the database

Escalation timers survive a restart. Nothing waits in memory.

Python 3.12, FastAPI, Postgres with PostGIS, APScheduler, Claude Haiku with structured output, Leaflet. 480 automated tests, every outside service mocked.

What is real today.

Working

  • Report, triage, merge, route, escalate and close the loop, end to end
  • Claude triage on a real street-dog photo (24 Nov)
  • Bite flow, camp booking, STATUS and CANCEL
  • Officials' dashboard with audit trail

Built, not live yet

  • WhatsApp: receiving works; Meta blocks replies until the business is verified
  • Telegram bot, Bhashini voice, Google Calendar: tested against stand-ins
  • Konkani, Marathi and Hindi: machine-drafted, awaiting a fluent reader

Placeholders

  • Every team and phone number is invented
  • Service areas are approximate circles
  • The health centre list is unconfirmed
  • No accuracy or time saving is claimed

Triage is guidance from a photo, not a diagnosis. In our live test the same photo was rated "needs attention soon" once and "not urgent" once, which is why routing never depends on it and a trained team always decides.
